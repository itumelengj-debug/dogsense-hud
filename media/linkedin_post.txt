What if your glasses could read your dog?

Dogs talk constantly through posture, tail and ears, and through pitch and rhythm. We usually catch only part of it. So I built DogSense HUD, a Symbolic Synapse prototype for the Rokid RV101 glasses that reads both channels at once and shows what it measures on the green heads-up display.

How it works:
Body language: a pose model (YOLO26n-pose, fine-tuned on 8,476 dog images) tracks 24 keypoints per dog. Everything is measured in the dog's own body frame, so my head can move and the reading still holds.
Sound: pitch, harmonicity and the rhythm of barks. An audio transformer checks every sound first, so my own voice is ignored.
Reasoning: explicit, explainable rules decide, not a black box. A play bow overrides a growl, and stiff + leaning forward + low growl triggers a warning. Every state on the HUD traces back to the cues that fired it.

The part I'm proudest of is the testing. I filmed my own dogs through the glasses and let reality grade it:
- Dogs facing me behind a gate were tracked in only 35% of frames. A second detection stage took that to 79%.
- A lying-down dog was read as a "play bow". Fixed.
- My "hey, hey, hey!" telling the dogs off was logged as barking. That one stung, and it led to the voice filter.
10 issues found in the field, 10 fixed.

It's honest about its limits: it reports what it saw and heard, not what a dog is thinking. The behaviour models still need fine-tuning on labelled real-world footage, and that's the next step.

Measured, never imagined.
